# Turnbuckle Alley: shared context for writers

This is the agreed design so far. It comes from three rounds of brainstorming with the game's creator. Treat everything here as canon. Expand and deepen it, never contradict it.

## The game
Turnbuckle Alley is a cozy, Stardew-Valley-style life sim set in small-town professional wrestling. It plays in the browser on desktop, iPad and phone. The edges are VERY whimsical (the Dungeon, the fairgrounds, the woods, the Mothman) and the heart is grounded. It is for newcomers and lifelong wrestling fans alike: references come in layers and are never gatekept. What the creator loves most about wrestling is the spectacle, the storylines and the community.

## The prologue (city life)
The player works at MaxxMedia, a giant national wrestling streaming company in the city. Their job is cutting matches into 9-second clips for an algorithm. A paper letter arrives from Grandma Dottie "The Duchess" Dupree: *"Go see Birdie. Learn everything she knows. Don't tell her I sent you."* Inside are a Polaroid of the Velvet Hammers, a bus ticket, and the key to Grandma's old house in Turnbuckle Alley. She never sold the house; she paid the taxes on it for 40 years. The player takes the bus.

## The main story
Dottie and Birdie Malone were the Velvet Hammers, the greatest tag team of the territory era.

**1983, "The Night of the Broken Belt."** A national company wanted only Birdie, and Birdie refused to go without Dottie. Dottie knew Birdie would give up the chance of a lifetime for her, so she changed the plan in the middle of the match. The planned breakup storyline had Birdie turning on Dottie. Instead, Dottie turned on Birdie. She broke their championship belt in half over her and left town that night without a word, so that Birdie would hate her and go.

Birdie didn't go. Heartbroken and stubborn, she stayed and spent 40 years keeping the Sportatorium alive. Each of them gave everything for the other, and both of them lost. Neither knows the truth. Each still has her half of the belt.

**Grandma is starting to forget.** Her memory loss is handled gently and with dignity. She remembers every match move by move, but not breakfast, and some days she calls the player "Birdie." She wants Birdie to know the truth while she can still remember it.

**The secrets they kept.** Sweet Lou has secretly mailed Dottie a tape of every Turnbuckle Alley show for 40 years. Birdie has kept Dottie's locker shut for 40 years, and the robe still hangs inside.

**How it unfolds.** Midway through the game, Grandma moves into the town retirement home three blocks away. The two women still won't see each other. The player brings Grandma old tapes, watching them brings her memories back, and the truth comes out piece by piece. When Birdie finally learns it, she is furious that anyone would give that up for her, and then she breaks.

**The final match** is at Homecoming: the player and a partner against the Velvet Hammers. The player ALWAYS loses, so the Velvet Hammers win their last match. Grandma remembers every move in the ring ("the body remembers the match"). Their finisher is the double-team move they were saving for the 1983 rematch that never happened. Birdie pins the player while the whole town counts along, and the two halves of the belt come back together.

**After the credits,** Grandma lives in town and sits in the front row every Saturday next to Agnes. She won't always know who you are, but she stands up every time your entrance music hits. The game continues forever.

## Kayfabe is an absolute law
To everyone who isn't a wrestling insider, wrestling is completely real, and nothing ever breaks that. Nobody slips and nobody gets caught.

In public, insiders stay in character automatically, and townsfolk react to storylines as real events. The bakery charges villains extra. Kids boo villains on the sidewalk. Sheriff Bev keeps offering to arrest the guy who hit you with a chair.

Insiders can only be themselves in insider spaces: the back booth at the Hot Tag Diner, the locker room, and Birdie's office. The only way anyone learns the truth is by joining the business (training to wrestle or joining the crew).

Insiders are the wrestlers, referees, ring crew and Birdie. Everyone else is a mark.

## Career
The player starts at the bottom of Birdie's promotion, Alley Championship Wrestling (ACW). They are NOT the booker at first. Earning "the pencil" (the right to book the shows) is the prize.

The ladder runs:
1. Opener
2. Undercard
3. Midcard
4. Main event
5. Assistant booker (books the Wednesday shows)
6. The Pencil (books everything)
7. Owner (buys the Sportatorium)
8. Founder of new wrestling towns, where the player delegates to protégés, trades talent between towns and stages invasions from one town to another

Early in the game, the big city pulls at people: MaxxMedia tempts wrestlers like Dex to leave. A rival promotion arrives late in the game.

## Shows
**Wednesdays** are small and rowdy: the VFW Hall, about 50 folding chairs, bingo afterward. **Saturdays** are big: the Sportatorium, with lights, pyro and confetti. Wrestling is optional, but the game rewards it.

Shows work like Stardew festivals. The player can wander, visit the concessions and their merch table, sign autographs, plan the finish backstage, watch matches, run in on someone's match, help the crew, or sit at the commentary desk. After each show, the insiders hang out in the Hot Tag back booth, which is where most story pitches happen.

Seasonal supershows are the festivals:
- Spring: Thaw Brawl
- Summer: Fairgrounds Fury
- Fall: Harvest Havoc
- Winter: Homecoming, with Hall of Fame inductions

All audio is chiptune and nothing is voiced.

## Matches
Matches are a Slay the Spire-like card game, and the player's moveset is their deck. The opponent is a scene partner who "calls" their next spot ahead of time, the way intents are shown in Slay the Spire.

There is no health bar. The match moves through story phases: feeling out → shine → heat → comeback → near-falls → finish. The crowd meter is the score. The AI steers toward the booked finish, so you usually end up there together. Match quality is a star rating.

Chemistry with friends makes their calls clearer. Gear works like relics. Match types change the rules.

## Storylines
There are hundreds of storylines, each with variations. Residents come to the player with ideas, and they talk it over, so the player is nearly building their own story.

**How much control the player wants is always their call.** Before each pitch, they choose:
- "You drive"
- "Let's build it together"
- "I've got an idea"

**A story fills these slots:** hook, who's the hero, twist, stakes, payoff match, length.

**How stories play out:**
- Story cards are collected from tapes, insiders' stories and life.
- Characters' personalities push back on ideas.
- A story runs in three acts over several weeks, with beats at the shows and "town moments" where marks react.
- The crowd can steer a story, which can cause someone to turn hero or villain.
- Flops are gentle: the Turnbuckle Tattler calls it a snooze, and the player can play a twist card to save it.

**Real life bleeds into the stories.** A romance can go on-screen or stay secret. A real fight can become a worked shoot (a staged storyline built on real tension). A real comeback from injury can become the story.

**Tone:** incredibly emotional is welcome. There is NO DEATH, ever, and nothing too evil. Lots and lots of variation.

## Activities (wrestling-heavy)
- **The Dungeon** (the equivalent of Stardew's mines): an impossibly deep training basement under the Sportatorium. Each floor is a gym from a different era. The player works the equipment like rocks in a mine and collects loot. Ghost wrestlers challenge them to quick sparring card matches, and the Golden Belt waits at the bottom.
- **Old tapes** (the equivalent of fishing): the player digs through VHS bins at the flea market, yard sales, the pawn shop, Fenwick's back room, the library and the dump. Watching a tape is a VHS-tracking mini-game where the player "catches" moments: move cards, story cards, promo lines, chants, gear designs and clues about 1983. Some tapes are legendary.
- **The backyard training yard** at Grandma's house (the equivalent of the farm), with merch machines (the equivalent of Stardew's artisan machines). Trainees come later.
- **Foraging** for folding steel chairs. Each chair is another seat at the shows.
- **Flyering**, and radio promos on WRSL 1340 AM.
- **Trading cards** to collect.
- **Gear and belts:** Marigold sews gear and Hank builds belts, both to the player's designs.
- **Training meals** and **fan mail**.
- **The fairgrounds**, home of Wanda the bear.
- **Romance:** every romanceable character is available whatever the player's gender, and all of them are adults. There is a wedding in the ring, and your spouse becomes your tag partner.

The town grows over time: new shops open and fans move in.

Skills: Strength, Ring IQ, Charisma, Craft, Storytelling.

## The cast seed
Names are canon unless you have a clearly better idea.

### Insiders
- **Birdie Malone** runs the shows. She's in her 70s and is half of the Velvet Hammers. She's warm ("sugar"), knows every carny trick and sneak, and protects you.
- **La Mariposa Dorada (Rosa Villanueva)** owns the taqueria. She's a masked luchadora carrying her abuela's mask lineage.
- **Big Earl "The Mountain" Odom** is a 6'8" monster villain in the ring and the gentle town librarian outside it. He's secretly writing a children's book.
- **Dex "Dropkick" Delgado** is a scrappy high-flyer who works at the gas station and dreams of MaxxMedia. His story lets him leave and come home.
- **"Gorgeous" Gideon Price** is a vain villain in sequins who is anxious in real life. He runs the hair salon and will never lose his hair.
- **The Bruiser Twins, Bo and Buck,** run Steel Chair Hardware and are always one argument away from splitting up.
- **Hazel "Hurricane" Huang** is a former champion coming back from a knee injury. She runs a physical therapy and yoga studio.
- **"Cowboy" Clint Ransom** is on his last run. His teenage daughter is a mark who hates his masked alter ego.
- **The Mothman** only appears at night. Even the insiders don't know who it is. Needs a satisfying secret identity, whimsical but grounded.
- **Tiny Tallbridge** is a 7-foot gentle-giant baker who bakes tiny cakes.
- **"Professor" Pinfall** is a mat-wrestling genius who teaches math at the high school.
- **Sweet Lou Bastian** is a 70s legend who lives in an Airstream trailer by Chokeslam Creek and fishes. He keeps the Dungeon key and was there in 1983.
- **Referee Mo** is also the mail carrier.
- **Gus Gravel** is the ring announcer and the WRSL DJ.
- **June Oyelaran** owns the Hot Tag Diner. She was a valet and manager in the 80s.
- **Hank** builds rings and does carpentry.
- **Marigold** is the seamstress who makes gear.
- **Doc Halloran** is the chiropractor who patches everyone up.

### Marks
- **Pip** is a 10-year-old superfan with a cardboard belt. Never romanceable.
- **Agnes Pickett** is 80, sits in the front row at every show, and hits villains with her purse.
- **Sheriff Bev** keeps trying to arrest villains.
- **Coach Patty Kowalski** coaches amateur wrestling at the high school. She is sure pro wrestling is fake and keeps trying to prove it. She never succeeds.
- **Clementine** edits the Turnbuckle Tattler and reviews the shows.
- **Fenwick** deals tapes at the flea market and has theories about the Mothman.
- **Mayor Delphine Oakes** wants to put the town on the map.
- **Nadia** is the new veterinarian, and she thinks your feuds are real.
- **Wanda** is a very polite wrestling bear at the fairgrounds who loves honey.
- **Jobber** is a raccoon who lives in the announce booth.

### Others
- Grandma Dottie "The Duchess" Dupree.
- The prologue's city characters at MaxxMedia.

## Places
Turnbuckle Alley (the town), the Sportatorium, the Hot Tag Diner, Steel Chair Hardware, the VFW Hall, WRSL 1340 AM, the Fairgrounds, Chokeslam Creek, Taqueria Mariposa, the Turnbuckle Tattler, the flea market, the retirement home, and the water tower shaped like a turnbuckle.
