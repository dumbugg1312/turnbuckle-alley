Where this bible adds something new (a surname, a backstory detail, a clue about 1983), it is an expansion of canon. Items marked **[Story team call]** touch the main story directly and should be confirmed before they're built.*

## Part 1: How this cast works

Turnbuckle Alley is a town of about two thousand people, one water tower shaped like a turnbuckle, and one enormous secret. Everybody here loves wrestling. A few dozen of them know how it's made.

The cast splits along that line.

**Insiders** are the wrestlers, the referees, the ring crew, and Birdie. They know wrestling is a performance built by people who trust each other with their bodies. In public they stay in character always, without thinking about it, the way a fish doesn't think about water.

**Marks** are everyone else. To a mark, wrestling is real. Not real with a wink: real. The Dust Devil really did throw sand in Cowboy Clint's legacy, the Mountain really is a menace, and the Duchess really did break Birdie Malone's heart in 1983. Marks are not fools. They are people who believe in something with their whole chest, and the game treats that belief as a form of love.

### The three rooms

Insiders can be their real selves in exactly three places: **the back booth at the Hot Tag Diner, the locker room, and Birdie's office** (including the ladder from her office up to the Sportatorium roof). Everywhere else, they're on.

This rule is a gift to the writing, not a limit on it. Some of the best scenes in this bible come from the strain of staying in character: Gideon fighting tears at his own salon and blaming the hairspray, La Mariposa never once speaking in public, the Mountain running the quietest storytime in America because the children are a little afraid of him. A heart event in public can still be emotional. The character just has to carry the feeling inside the persona, and the player gets to see the crack.

> **Proposal:** the Dungeon is reachable only through the locker room and opened only by Sweet Lou's key, so treat it as part of the locker room. Several heart events below take place on its upper floors. **[Story team call]**

### Kayfabe in practice

- **There is no slip, ever.** Nobody gets caught, nobody overhears, nobody finds the script. The game has no mechanic for it and no writer should invent one.
- **Even memory can't break it.** Grandma Dottie forgets breakfast, but she has never once broken kayfabe, and never will. The business lives deeper in her than the date does.
- **Marks learn the truth only by joining the business.** In this cast that happens through training (Lacey, someday), joining the crew (Arlo, Sami), or a romance's *Behind the Curtain* event. Birdie decides who comes through the curtain, every time.
- **Marks are always sincere.** Never write a mark as stupid for believing. Write them as people who care. Sheriff Bev isn't a joke because she wants to arrest the Dust Devil; she's funny because she's *right*, by her own lights, and she keeps showing up with the paperwork.
- **Emotional truth is not business truth.** An insider can say "I'm sorry" or "I miss her" anywhere, because those things are true inside the story too. What they can't say outside the three rooms is how the story is made.

### Casting principles

1. **Range.** Ages run from 10 to 80, plus a bear and a raccoon. Bodies run from five-foot Odessa Pruitt to seven-foot Tiny Tallbridge, from wiry Dex to 340-pound Earl. The cast is Cajun, Irish, Mexican-American, Puerto Rican, Black Southern, Nigerian-American, Taiwanese-American, Filipino-American, Tamil-American, Afghan-American, Lebanese-American, Hungarian-American, Polish-American, Oklahoman, Wisconsin farm stock, and more. Genders include women, men, and a nonbinary character among the romance options. Families include two dads, a sheriff and her wife, a single father and his teenage daughter, twin brothers who share a store and a temper, and a luchadora who lives above her taqueria with the grandmother whose mask she wears.
2. **Every villain is kind underneath.** The Mountain is writing a children's book. Gorgeous Gideon knits. The Dust Devil is a fifty-two-year-old dad who can't stop loving the thing that nearly hurt him. Professor Pinfall gives the most generous extra credit in the state.
3. **Every mark has dignity and a life beyond wrestling.** Each one would keep living a full life if wrestling vanished tomorrow (it won't). Their belief shapes their days, but it isn't all they are.
4. **No death, ever.** Not in backstory, not offscreen, not for pets. When someone leaves, they move to Arizona and send postcards. The emotional tools are retirement, distance, illness handled gently, estrangement, reconciliation, aging, and the slow work of saying true things out loud.
5. **Humor never punches down.** We laugh with people at what they love and at the situations kayfabe creates. Never at a body, a background, an accent, or a memory.
6. **Everyone gets a cry and a laugh.** Each character carries at least one storyline that could make a player cry and one that could make them laugh.

### Romance in Turnbuckle Alley

Eleven adults close to the player's age are romanceable, whatever the player's gender: seven insiders and four marks.

- **Dating** begins at 8 hearts with a **Ringside Pair**: two front-row tickets that Birdie hand-punches at the box office (she punches a little heart instead of a hole, and pretends she didn't).
- **Public dates with insiders happen in character.** Dating the Mountain in public means he orders tea like a threat. Dating Mariposa means charades. The back booth is where you finally get to talk.
- **The Hot Tag (insiders).** At 10 hearts, an insider partner asks the canon question: does the romance go on-screen as a storyline, or stay a secret? Both paths are full and complete.
- **Behind the Curtain (marks).** Your spouse becomes your tag partner, so a mark can only marry the player by joining the business. With Birdie's blessing, the player brings them through the curtain, and they learn the truth. Each mark romance's reaction to that moment is its biggest scene, and each one is different.
- **The proposal** uses **the Tag Rope**: a braided turnbuckle tassel, Hank's rope wound with Marigold's thread. You hold out your hand on the apron, the way a wrestler reaches for the tag.
- **The wedding is in the ring.** Mayor Delphine handles the legal part. Gus announces the couple like a main event. Referee Mo counts to three for the kiss.

### Reading an entry

Each entry gives role, side (insider or mark), age, pronouns, romance status, art notes, personality, ring details (insiders) or belief and life (marks), gifts, a weekly sketch, relationships, heart events at 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, romance beats where relevant, three storyline seeds, any tie to 1983, a leitmotif, and sample lines.

- **Show days** are Wednesday (VFW Hall, 50 folding chairs, bingo after) and Saturday (the Sportatorium, lights and pyro). Off days are everything else.
- **Storyline seeds** are tagged **[Cry]**, **[Laugh]** or **[Spectacle]**. They're starting hands; every one can be run "You drive," "Let's build it together," or "I've got an idea."
- **Lines** are marked *Public* (in character, kayfabe intact), *Private* (real self, in one of the three rooms), and *Loved gift*. Marks have no kayfabe to drop, so their *Private* lines are heart-to-hearts: the moment the guard comes down.
- **Leitmotifs** are chiptune. Each theme plays relaxed around town and becomes that character's entrance music at shows.

## Part 2: The Velvet Hammers

The greatest tag team of the territory era, apart for forty years and three blocks. Their themes are written to be played together: Dottie's is a waltz, Birdie's is a shuffle, and the Velvet Hammers' theme moves both into 6/8, where a waltz and a shuffle can finally dance.

### 1. Dorothy "Dottie" Dupree · "The Duchess"

**Role:** The player's grandmother and the retired half of the Velvet Hammers. She lives in the city at the start and moves into the Evening Bell Residence, three blocks from the Sportatorium, midway through the game.
**Side:** Insider (retired, and still the most devoted keeper of kayfabe in the cast)
**Age:** 76 · **Pronouns:** she/her · **Romanceable:** No

**Look (art notes):** Small, straight-backed, and built like someone who was taught to walk to the ring as if she owned it. Silver hair in her famous crown braid (she can still do it herself on good days; Gideon does it on the others). A lavender cardigan with velvet elbow patches she sewed on herself. A pearl earring in one ear and a rhinestone stud in the other, rescued from her 1983 robe. Reading glasses on a beaded chain, white sneakers with plum laces. **Signature colors:** plum, antique gold, cream. **Distinguishing features:** a crooked left pinky (broken in 1979, never set right, "a souvenir"), a fine white scar through her right eyebrow, and a wave she does with the back of her hand that the whole town still boos on reflex.

**Personality:** Dry, regal, mischievous, and generous. She grew up on Bayou Lafourche, learned to wrestle at a traveling carnival at sixteen, and became the showboat of the Velvet Hammers: Birdie threw the punches, Dottie made the crowd beg for them. She calls everyone *cher*. She cheats at gin rummy and confesses only when she wins. She sings Cajun waltzes in French while she does dishes, badly, on purpose, to make people laugh. She loves the player fiercely and shows it by bossing them.

**Persona vs. real self:** To the town, the Duchess is the villain of the century: the woman who broke the championship belt over Birdie Malone and vanished. Dottie lets them think so. In public she still plays the part, chin up, gracious, faintly wicked. In private she's tired, funny, frightened of forgetting, and still devoted to her best friend with the kind of love that has never needed a name.

**Memory, handled with care**

- She remembers every match move by move, and not breakfast. Some days she calls the player "Birdie." This is canon, and it is never played for laughs.
- **Good days and foggy days.** Her state is quiet and daily, never a meter, never a countdown. On good days there are stories, jokes and rummy. On foggy days she may believe it's 1979 and she's getting ready for a show. She may ask where her boots are. She may call the player Bird.
- **There is no wrong answer.** On foggy days every dialogue option is kind: *play along*, *gently remind her*, or *just sit with her*. The game never grades the player. It only shows how she responds, and she always responds to warmth. Sami teaches the player the rule early: "Don't correct. Connect."
- **The humor in her scenes comes from her wit, never her confusion.** She is the funniest person in the room on purpose.
- **The tapes are the bridge.** Watching an old match together brings her all the way back for a while: sometimes an hour, sometimes a whole evening. That's when the truth about 1983 surfaces.
- **The body remembers.** Her muscle memory is untouched. She can't recall the nurse's name, but she can call a forty-minute match spot by spot and catch a missed cue from across the room.
- **Kayfabe lives deeper than memory.** On her foggiest day, if a mark walks in, the Duchess sits up straighter. At Evening Bell she speaks emotional truth freely ("I'm sorry," "I miss her") because it's true inside the story too. The business truth she saves for the back booth.

**Life at Evening Bell**

- **Room 7.** She chooses it for the window, which faces the Sportatorium marquee. She can read the Saturday card from her bed, and she does, every Saturday, and asks Sami to tell her if Birdie's name is on it. It always is.
- **She arranges the room like a locker room.** Cardigans on hooks, sneakers lined up toes out, a vanity mirror with a ring of bulbs Hank rewired for her. Under the bed sits a battered hatbox she checks every night. Inside, wrapped in a plum scarf, is her half of the belt. Some nights she forgets why it's there. She never forgets that it is.
- **The memory book.** Sami starts a scrapbook on her first day. The player adds photos from shows and tapes. Each page has a picture and a caption in Dottie's handwriting; later in Sami's when her hand gets tired; sometimes in the player's.
- **The labeled drawers.** Sami labels them, then she relabels them: *CARDIGANS. NOT FOR BORROWING, LAVINIA.*
- **The weekly rhythm.**
  - **Monday:** hair day. Gideon visits in full Gorgeous character and treats her like visiting royalty, which she is.
  - **Tuesday:** gin rummy in the sunroom. She "works the table" against Velma, Lavinia and Farid, and wins in ways that cannot be explained by luck.
  - **Wednesday:** the VFW show on WRSL, on a transistor radio held to her good ear.
  - **Thursday:** chair yoga with Hazel, which Dottie narrates like a title match ("She's going for the stretch... the Duchess REVERSES").
  - **Friday:** dance night. Sami plays ukulele; she waltzes with Farid, who has adored the Duchess as a villain since 1975 and is thrilled to have his toes stepped on by her.
  - **Saturday:** she watches the marquee lights come on at dusk. Later in the game she sits in the front row.
  - **Sunday:** tape night with the player. Sami wheels in the old AV cart, the VCR clicks, and she comes back.
- **She calls the activities director "the promoter"** and negotiates for better pudding. She manages the residents' quarrels like a heel manager, which makes the feuds at Evening Bell the most entertaining in the county.
- **The neighbors.** Velma Ruiz (90), the seamstress who made the Velvet Hammers' robes. Lavinia Beaulieu (94), founder of the Turnbuckle Tattler and Clementine's great-aunt. Farid Haddad (89), Sami's grandfather, a lifelong mark and her favorite person to lose to on purpose (she never does).
- **The walk.** On good afternoons she walks one block, to the corner where she can see the Sportatorium's back door. She stands there a while. Then she turns around.

**Gifts.** *Loves:* VHS tapes of old territory shows (her own matches most of all), a fresh deck of cards, pralines, chicory coffee, anything plum, a photo of the Sportatorium marquee lit up. *Likes:* lavender sachets, large-print crosswords, Tiny's petits fours, gardenias, old programs, Polaroids of anyone in the crowd wearing a homemade sign. *Dislikes:* being helped before she asks, beige clothing, MaxxMedia anything, and being quizzed ("Do you remember me?" makes her chin go up like a drawbridge).

**Schedule.** *Before the move:* in the city; she calls the Hot Tag's payphone every Sunday at 6 p.m., and the player can answer. *After the move:* the Evening Bell rhythm above. Wednesday by the radio, Saturday by the window (later, the front row). On good days the player can walk her to the Hot Tag's back booth, where June sets out chicory coffee and two spoons of sugar without asking. It's the only place in town she can say the whole truth out loud.

**Relationships.** *Birdie:* her other half; forty years apart, three blocks apart. *The player:* her grandchild, and on foggy days, also Birdie. *Sweet Lou:* her oldest friend and keeper of her secret; forty years of tapes. *June:* drove her to the bus in 1983 and still has her boots. *Agnes:* hit her with a purse in 1983; becomes her front-row seatmate. *Sami:* her nurse and favorite audience. *Gideon:* does her braid. *Hazel:* she calls her "Typhoon," and Hazel lets her. *Doc:* the referee who counted it. *Velma, Lavinia, Farid:* her rummy marks.

**Heart events** *(interlocked with the main story; the story team sets the exact gating)*
- **2♥:** Sunday, 6 p.m., the diner payphone rings. She wants to know everything: is the water tower still that shape, does June still burn the toast, does Birdie still wear "that awful jacket." Then, quietly: "Don't tell her I asked."
- **4♥:** Moving day. She refuses help with exactly one thing, the hatbox. She picks Room 7, sits on the bed, reads the Saturday card off the marquee aloud, and says, "Well. There she is."
- **6♥:** The first tape night. She narrates a 1978 match move by move, mouthing the calls before they happen and laughing at a slip nobody else would notice. Afterward she can't remember whether she ate dinner. Sami brings pudding. She wins his from him at rummy.
- **8♥:** A foggy afternoon. She calls you Bird, pats the bed for you to sit, and says what she has never said: "You were supposed to go. Why didn't you go?" The player can take her hand. She falls asleep holding it. It's the first piece of the truth, said to the wrong person, who is also somehow the right one.
- **10♥:** A good day, bright and clear. She opens the hatbox, unwraps her half of the belt, and says, "Train me, cher. One more. The body remembers the match, even when the rest of me gets lost on the way there." This opens the Homecoming training arc.

**Storyline seeds**
1. **[Laugh] The Duchess Holds Court.** Dottie becomes the player's on-screen manager, seated on a velvet throne at ringside, issuing royal decrees ("This match shall be contested in a more flattering light"). She can't always remember the card, but she's never forgotten a heel tactic, and the crowd boos her with forty years of affection.
2. **[Cry] Heir to the Velvet.** In the backyard ring, on a good day, she teaches the player the Curtsy, her drop-toe hold disguised as a bow. She has to teach it three Sundays running because she forgets she already has, and the player lets her, every time. When the player finally debuts it on a Saturday, she stands up in the front row and curtsies back.
3. **[Spectacle] The Encore.** The canon Homecoming final: the player and a partner against the Velvet Hammers. The player always loses. Dottie calls every spot from memory, and they finish with the Encore, the double-team they were saving for a 1983 rematch that never happened.

**Ring notes (finale and flashbacks).** *Style:* technical showboat; she stalled, baited, then out-wrestled everyone. *Signature:* **The Curtsy** (she sinks into a curtsy, hooks the ankles, and the opponent face-plants into her bow). *Finisher:* **The Royal Decree** (a bridging cradle pin; she raises one hand in a queenly wave while the referee counts). *Team finisher with Birdie:* **The Encore** (Dottie trips the opponent with the Curtsy, Birdie rolls them into a cradle, Dottie holds the legs, and they finish with their hands joined across the opponent's chest). *Entrance, 1983:* a velvet curtain drop and a waltz. *Entrance, Homecoming:* the same waltz, slower, in the plum robe from the locker. *Alignment:* in town memory, the greatest villain who ever lived.

**1983 tie:** She is the truth. She holds why she did it, her half of the belt, and the fact that she watched every show for forty years on Lou's tapes. She does not know that Birdie went to the bus depot that night, that Birdie turned the national company down a second time, or that Birdie's kept her locker shut with the robe inside. See Part 9.

**Leitmotif: "The Duchess Waltz."** A Cajun waltz in 3/4: a pulse-wave accordion over a triangle-wave fiddle, 84 bpm, with a little ornamental trill on each downbeat like a curtsy. Around Evening Bell it plays soft and slightly slow, as if someone's humming it from memory. On foggy days, one phrase drops out and the melody waits for it, then carries on. At Homecoming it becomes her entrance at full tempo, and then it meets Birdie's.

**Lines**
- *Public (Evening Bell sunroom):* "Gin. Again. I'd apologize, Farid, but the Duchess never apologizes. It's in my contract."
- *Private (the back booth):* "Forty years I watched her on Lou's tapes, cher. Every Saturday. She got old on me one VHS at a time."
- *Loved gift (an old tape):* "Thaw Brawl '81! She wore green that night and everybody said it was bad luck. Sit, sit. I'll tell you every move before it happens."
- *Foggy day:* "Bird? You're early. Help me with my braid. My hands won't do what I tell them today."
- *Disliked gift (anything beige):* "Beige. Well. I'll wear it the day I retire from being looked at, cher."

### 2. Bernadette "Birdie" Malone

**Role:** Runs Alley Championship Wrestling, the Sportatorium, and, depending on who you ask, the town. Half of the Velvet Hammers.
**Side:** Insider · **Age:** 74 · **Pronouns:** she/her · **Romanceable:** No

**Look (art notes):** Five-foot-two and shaped like a fire hydrant that learned to hug. White hair in a short wave with one stubborn curl of the old auburn at the nape. Rhinestone cat-eye glasses on a chain. An oversized crimson velvet blazer worn shiny at the cuffs (cut down from her last ring robe), an ACW T-shirt, jeans, and white ring boots scuffed gray. A ring of about forty keys on her belt loop. A chewed yellow carpenter's pencil behind her ear: **the Pencil**, which has written every ACW card since 1984. A cinnamon stick in the corner of her mouth (she quit smoking in 1988). A hearing aid Marigold bedazzled for her birthday. **Signature colors:** crimson, gold, ring-canvas white. **Distinguishing features:** a nose broken three times, a gap tooth, and forearms that still look like they could open a pickle jar by staring at it.

**Personality:** Warm, blunt, and wise to every hustle. She calls everyone "sugar." She can spot a crooked promoter's tell from across a parking lot and a scared rookie's from across the building. She protects her people first, pays them before herself, and sleeps on her office couch more nights than she admits. She sings off-key at the top of her lungs while she sweeps the ring. She has never watched the tape of the 1983 match, and she never will, thank you, sugar, and pass the coffee.

**Persona vs. real self:** In public she's the Commissioner: beloved by fans, a terror to villains. She "fines" the Bruiser Twins, "suspends" the Dust Devil, and once "banned" the Mountain from the building for a week (he worked the library harder). In private she's tender and tired, and she keeps the light on for someone who left forty years ago.

**Ring notes.** *Style (1970 to 1983):* brawler; Birdie threw the punches while Dottie made them beg. *Signature:* **The Birdcage** (a crossface with the opponent's arms trapped behind her knees). *Finisher:* **Hammer Down** (a double axe-handle off the second rope). *Team finisher with Dottie:* **The Encore** (never performed). *Entrance today:* when the Commissioner comes out, the house lights go red and the crowd stands without being asked. *Alignment:* hero, and the town's moral center.

**Gifts.** *Loves:* cinnamon sticks, June's coffee (black), a photo of a sold-out crowd, territory-era programs, red velvet cake, a flyer put up in a new town. *Likes:* folding chairs (she counts them), ticket rolls, carnival glass, honky-tonk records, peppermints. *Dislikes:* MaxxMedia merch, phones held up during a match, anything called "content," and lilies ("smells like a promoter's office").

**Schedule.** *Saturday:* the Sportatorium by 10 a.m.; the stool by the curtain during the show; the back booth after, until June turns the lights off on her. *Wednesday:* the VFW Hall by 4 p.m., working the door herself. *Off days:* her office 8 to noon; lunch at the Hot Tag counter, same stool, never the back booth by daylight; afternoons flyering or arguing with the bank. *Monday mornings:* fishing with Lou at Chokeslam Creek. *Sunday evenings:* her porch, which has two rocking chairs. She only ever sits in one.

**Relationships.** *Dottie:* the love of her life in the way only a partner of fifteen years can be; the wound she won't name. *The player:* her protégé, her hope, eventually her successor. *Sweet Lou:* her Monday fishing partner, keeping a secret that sits on him like a stone. *June:* her best friend in the present; they bicker like an old married couple. *Gus:* drove her to the depot that night. *Hank:* changed the lock on the locker, no questions asked. *Doc:* refereed the match; she's never blamed him, and he's never stopped blaming himself. *Mo:* her right hand at shows. *Agnes:* her oldest fan, who has had seat A1 free since 1984. *Royce Penn:* she can smell a corporate lanyard from the county line. *Mayor Delphine:* they spar over permits and adore each other.

**Heart events**
- **2♥:** She teaches you the carny flyer fold, the one that lands face-up on any windshield in any wind. "First rule, sugar: a flyer nobody reads is a napkin."
- **4♥:** She lets you sit on the stool by the curtain for one match and makes you close your eyes. "Hear that? That's a room breathing. Learn to hear it and you'll never need a script."
- **6♥:** After a thin house, you find her alone, counting chairs. She tells you about a Saturday in 1985 when eleven people came. She ran the whole show anyway, main event and all. "Eleven people paid. Eleven people got a show."
- **8♥:** She walks you past a padlocked locker with the nameplate scraped off. She doesn't stop. She only says, "Don't."
- **10♥:** Dawn, up the ladder from her office to the Sportatorium roof, where she grows tomatoes in old ring buckets. She tells you the thing she's never said out loud: she went to the bus depot that night. "I was going to drag her back by the braid. Bus was gone. Five minutes, Gus says. I've been five minutes late for forty years."

**Storyline seeds**
1. **[Spectacle] The Commissioner Swings.** A villain stable (late game, the rival promotion's invaders are ideal) lays hands on Birdie in the ring. She takes off her glasses, hands them to Mo, climbs to the second rope at seventy-four, and drops Hammer Down. The Sportatorium nearly loses its roof.
2. **[Laugh] Sugar's Fines.** Commissioner Birdie's weekly fines become a segment, each sillier than the last: the Bruiser Twins must repaint the VFW Hall, Gideon must work a Saturday with his hair "slightly flat," the Mountain owes the town forty casseroles. Townsfolk start suggesting fines by mail.
3. **[Cry] Forty Years of Chairs.** An anniversary show. Birdie invites every fan who ever bought a ticket, and the eleven people from 1985 sit in the front row. Her speech is about them, not her. The player books it, and has to talk her out of giving the night's gate away.

### 14. The Mothman

**Role:** A cryptid who appears only at night. Even the insiders don't know who it is. (The player can find out. See Part 8.)
**Side:** Insider (whoever it is, they know exactly how to work a match) · **Age:** unknown · **Pronouns:** the town says "it" · **Romanceable:** see Part 8

**Look (art notes):** A hunched, winged triangle with two glowing red eyes. A fuzzy gray-brown hooded bodysuit; enormous tattered wings in dusty brown with pale eye-spots, which open from the shoulders with a sweep of both arms; feathery antennae that bob when it tilts its head; red-lensed goggles that glow. When it moves, a faint shimmer of wing dust falls. **Signature colors:** moth brown, ash gray, porch-light amber, and two red dots. **Distinguishing features:** it never speaks. It tilts its head. It is always gone before the house lights come back up.

**Persona:** Curious, eerie, and oddly kind. It's drawn to light. It appears when a story needs a miracle and helps whoever the crowd most needs helped. Fenwick has filled three corkboards trying to explain it.

**The rules the town has noticed:** It only appears after dark. It never appears at the VFW ("the Mothman doesn't do Wednesdays"). It appears in the chaos of a match, very often while the referee is down. Dawn sightings cluster along the creek road and around the water tower.

**Ring notes.** *Style:* eerie and fluttering; it feints like a moth against a window, then strikes. *Signature:* **The Flutter** (a disorienting double wing-feint into an armdrag). *Finisher:* **Porch Light** (it lifts the opponent toward the hanging bulb, spins with them beneath it, and lowers them into a cradle pin under the light). *Entrance:* no music. The house lights dim to amber. A single bare bulb on a cord, hung over the ring for a 1979 "Haunted House" match and never taken down, clicks on by itself. A flutter of wings in the noise channel. Then something slowly unfolds from beneath the ring apron on the far side, like a moth leaving its cocoon. *Alignment:* a tweener (a wrestling word for someone between hero and villain); it serves the story, not a side.

**Gifts.** You can't hand the Mothman anything. But gifts left on the **Moth Stump**, a flat tree stump by the water tower where Fenwick leaves offerings, are gone by morning. *Loves:* a UV flashlight, a moth field guide, a jar of fairgrounds honey, a vintage postage stamp. *Likes:* porch-light bulbs, wool socks, shiny buttons. *Dislikes:* flash photography, bug zappers, anything with a tracking device hidden in it (Fenwick has tried, twice).

**Heart events (via the Moth Stump; this is the reveal path, detailed in Part 8)**
- **2♥:** Your gift is gone at dawn. In its place, a single "wing scale": a scrap of dusty brown felt.
- **4♥:** A note in block capitals: THANK YOU. Turn it over: it's written on the back of a junk-mail flyer.
- **6♥:** A paper moth, folded expertly from a postal "Sorry We Missed You" slip.
- **8♥:** A Saturday match goes badly for you. The porch light clicks on. The Mothman saves you, and in the dark, it squeezes your hand once.
- **10♥:** One last note on the stump: UNDER THE RING. SATURDAY. COME ALONE.

**Storyline seeds**
1. **[Spectacle] Wings vs. Mask.** Against La Mariposa Dorada (see Rosa). The Mothman's side of the stakes: if it loses, it leaves town forever. It can never be unmasked; see Part 8 for how that match ends.
2. **[Laugh] The Mothman's Cousin.** A villain (Gideon, wings sewn from salon capes) dresses as a counterfeit Mothman to cause chaos. The real one appears, and the two "Mothmen" do a perfect mirror routine across the ring before the real one hits Porch Light. The counterfeit's antennae are on backwards the whole time.
3. **[Cry] Two Thousand Porch Lights.** A storm knocks out the Sportatorium's power in the middle of Harvest Havoc. Nobody goes home. In the dark, the crowd raises flashlights and lighters, one by one, and the Mothman comes out and finishes the main event in the light of two thousand people who refused to leave.

**1983 tie:** None, which is part of why it's so beloved. It's the one story in town that isn't about the past.

**Leitmotif:** No entrance music, only silence, a noise-channel flutter, and one sustained note when the porch light clicks on. Around town after 10 p.m., near the water tower, a faint three-note phrase plays on a triangle wave. (Players who listen closely will eventually notice that its rhythm is a referee's three-count.)

**Lines**
- *Public (a dawn sighting):* *The Mothman tilts its head at you. Somewhere a porch light hums. When you blink, it's gone.*
- *A note on the Moth Stump:* "THANK YOU. THE HONEY WAS VERY GOOD. TELL THE BEAR."

- *Loved gift, left on the stump (a UV flashlight):* *At dawn the flashlight is gone. In its place, a paper moth with its wings spread wide, which is how moths look when they're happy.*

## Part 8: The Mothman, Secret Identity and Reveal Path

> **Spoilers for the whole game.** This section is for the team, not for the in-game encyclopedia.

### Who it is

**The Mothman is Referee Mo.** Maribel "Mo" Dizon: the town's mail carrier, ACW's senior referee, and an amateur moth surveyor. Her 5 a.m. route in a flapping orange poncho and a red-lens headlamp accidentally started the legend. She leaned into it by slipping under the ring during her own ref bumps, to become the one thing a referee is never allowed to be: the story.

### Why it works

- **It's grounded.** The cryptid is a real person with a real trick, and the trick is pure wrestling: the referee always gets knocked out right before the run-in. Everybody's seen it a thousand times. Nobody suspects the person lying on the floor.
- **It's whimsical.** The town's spookiest legend began as a mail carrier in a rain poncho, and the person under the wings counts moths for fun.
- **It explains the "impossible" things.** The Mothman always helps the booked finish, though Birdie swears she doesn't book it, because the referee is the one person who's always told the finish. It never appears at the VFW, because the VFW's ring sits on a bare floor with no crawlspace. It appears at dawn on the creek road, because that's where the route starts.
- **It means something.** Refs are supposed to be invisible, and Mo has been invisible her whole life: the steady middle child, the best referee in the territory, the person nobody remembers was there. For forty seconds a night, two thousand people can't take their eyes off her, and none of them know it's her. That's the part she loves.
- **It's fair.** Every clue is in plain sight from the first weeks of the game.

### How the legend began

Four years ago, Gus Gravel was driving to WRSL at 5:30 a.m. for *The Gravel Pit* when he saw two red eyes and something huge and flapping on the creek road in the fog. He told the entire county on the air that morning. Mo heard it on her truck radio and laughed so hard she missed a mailbox.

A few weeks later the Sportatorium drew its smallest Saturday crowd in a decade: 212 people. Mo, refereeing the main event, thought about those 212 people and the empty seats around them. The next week she sewed wings onto the poncho. By the end of the month she'd found the old crawlspace under the ring, and the 1979 porch-light switch, still wired, waiting.

Two weeks after the Mothman's first run-in, the Saturday crowd was 450. Birdie stood at the curtain with her cinnamon stick, said "Well, I'll be," and decided not to ask.

### How the trick works

1. A match is booked with a ref bump. Mo takes **the Sack of Mail**: through the ropes, to the floor, and a roll under the ring apron "to recover."
2. The Sportatorium ring sits over a double crawlspace, partitioned since the 1979 Haunted House match. Hank works the near side during shows; the far side has been sealed off for decades. Mo keeps the costume there, next to the old porch-light switch and the ladder hatch down to the Dungeon's top landing.
3. She flips the switch. The bulb over the ring clicks on. She unfolds from under the far apron like a moth leaving its cocoon.
4. She works the run-in: safe, precise, serving the booked finish (which, as referee, she knows).
5. Back under the apron. Out of the suit in forty seconds. She crawls out the near side, a dazed Referee Mo, in time to count.

Hank has noticed that someone keeps the porch-light circuit in perfect order. She has decided that's none of her business.

**Color, not evidence:** Abuela Celia's memory that Dottie said in 1979 she'd "never wrestle without Bird beside me." Farid Haddad's lifelong verdict: "The Duchess had a broken heart, not a black one." Coach Patty's certainty, at twelve, that it was fake because no real friend could do that. Sheriff Bev, sixteen, deciding bad people shouldn't get away with things.

### How it surfaces across the game

**Act I: before Grandma moves.** The player hears the town's version (the Duchess betrayed Birdie) and finds ambient clues that don't add up: Agnes's photo of a crying villain, the frozen departures board, Gus's ring card showing the finish was supposed to be Birdie's turn, Earl's rematch ads, Clementine's front page, a ruined tape nobody can fix, and Birdie walking past a padlocked locker without stopping. The question forms: *Dottie changed the finish. Why?*

**Act II: Grandma in town.** As hearts deepen and tape nights begin, the insiders open up: Lou's hallway, Doc's whisper, June's ride and the boots, Marigold's margin note, Hank's jewel, Mo's envelopes. Dottie, on good days, remembers in fragments: the phone, the bus, a city apartment, the first padded envelope arriving. On a foggy day she calls the player Bird and asks why she didn't go. The player now holds most of Dottie's half, and can't yet give it to Birdie, because it isn't theirs to give.

**Act III: the truth.** Arlo and Fenwick restore the tape. Odessa reads frame 4,410. Clementine finds page six. Gus, finally, tells the story of the depot. The player holds both halves. The story team stages the two deliveries: Birdie learns why, is furious that anyone would give that up for her, and then breaks. Dottie learns that Birdie went to the depot, said no twice, and kept the locker shut with the robe inside. Then the training for Homecoming begins.

### Kayfabe and the truth

This is the one place where the town's story and the true story nearly match. The town already believes the Duchess betrayed Birdie and broke her heart. When marks find pieces (Agnes's photo, Arlo's restored frames) they read them in kayfabe ("She regretted it!"), and those readings drive the town's side of the redemption. The business pieces (the ring card, the audible, the whisper, the plan) are spoken only in the three rooms. For the marks, Homecoming is the greatest redemption story in the history of the territory, and they're right.

### The Homecoming payoff map

Everything scattered comes back together on one night. A suggestion for the story team:

- **The robe** comes out of the locker; Marigold restores it, with Velma supervising.
- **The boots** come down from June's storeroom shelf; June laces them.
- **The braid** is Gideon's work, from the 1981 photo.
- **The belt:** Birdie's half from her desk drawer, Dottie's from the hatbox, rejoined by Hank and Marigold, with the rhinestone back in the center plate.
- **The referee** is Doc, in stripes for the first time in forty years. Mo hands him the count.
- **The front row:** Agnes in A1 and the original 1971 fans. The Evening Bell section, loud. Lou, who sings.
- **The crown:** Delphine, Miss Homecoming at last.
- **The call:** Gus, on Old Thunder.
- **The headline:** Clementine's, written before the bell, no question mark.
- **The finish:** the Encore. Birdie pins the player. The whole town counts.
