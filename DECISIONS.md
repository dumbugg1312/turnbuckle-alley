# Decisions

The creator went to bed on 2026-10-06 and asked for the game to be built and playtested overnight. These are the calls I made on my own, so they can be reviewed and reversed.

- **D-001 Stack.** Vite + TypeScript with no framework. The world is drawn on a Canvas2D at a low native resolution, scaled by a whole number so the pixels stay crisp. Menus, dialogue and cards are a DOM layer drawn over the canvas. Why: it runs in any browser (desktop, iPad, phone), there's no engine lock-in, and I can playtest it in the built-in browser.
- **D-002 Pixel-perfect scaling.** The scale factor is device-pixel height ÷ 220, rounded down. The view then fills the screen at that scale, so phones, iPads and desktops all show roughly 220–270 native pixels of height and nothing is stretched.
- **D-003 All art and audio are made in code.** No downloaded images or sound files. Sprites are palette-locked string grids plus procedural drawing; music and sound effects come from a WebAudio chiptune synth. Why: one consistent style, nothing licensed, and parametric characters for the deep character creator.
- **D-004 Pixel fonts are bundled through fontsource** (SIL Open Font License), so the game works offline.
- **D-005 Art direction: A, "Golden Hour Storybook"** (mockups/style-a.html). It was the first mockup finished and clearly the strongest fit for "detailed, charming, not Stardew, not Roblox": warm hue-shifted shading, selective outlines, plum ink instead of black, golden-hour small town. B and C were still rendering when I had to commit so the art helpers could start. They stay in mockups/ for comparison. The mockup's toolkit is ported to src/gfx/kit.ts and its fonts to src/gfx/font.ts.
- **D-006 The world is 3/4 top-down** with 16px tiles. Buildings face south with their front facades showing, so walking along Main Street looks like the mockup.
- **D-007 Matches are drawn on a half-resolution "stage"** scaled up 2x. On every screen the ring reads big and gets its own camera (zoom, shake, slow motion for finishers).
- **D-008 Match design.** The story phases (feeling out → shine → heat → comeback → stretch → finish) are the "enemy HP". Each phase has a goal, and the crowd meter is the score. The opponent telegraphs a "call" each turn, as Slay the Spire does with intents: setups reward certain card types; attacks reward sells. The booked finish always happens. Running out of gas skips to the finish instead of losing. Kickouts are a short timing tap: a 2.9 count is the big pop, and missing the tap just puts your foot on the ropes (it never loses the match). Tuned so story-aware play averages about 3.8★, random play about 2.2★, and 5★ is rare.
- **D-009 The match engine is pure logic** (src/match/engine.ts) with a seeded RNG, and is covered by a bot simulation test (tests/match-sim.test.ts). The scene (src/match/scene.ts) only animates its event log.
- **D-010 Paced the helpers after hitting the usage limit at about 11:50 PM.** On resume I ran fewer helpers at once, in priority order (characters → art → storylines → Dungeon and tapes → dialogue and chapters), and kept a reserve so the build would never be left half-broken.
- **D-011 Calls are never blurred.** Low chemistry still lowers the "called" bonus, but the tutorial needs the calls readable.
- **D-012 Interiors stay mostly lit at night** (cozy), with only a slight dimming.
- **D-013 The retirement home is the Evening Bell Residence and Grandma lives in Room 7,** following CAST.md. Pip is Pip Abernathy. Dex works at a gas station and Gideon at his salon; both buildings were added to Main Street and Second Street.
- **D-014 Tiny's storyline rule stays absolute:** a villain may mock her cakes, never her body.
- **D-015 HMR is off in Vite.** Many people were editing at once and auto-reloads broke playtests. Reload manually.
- **D-016 Art upgrade after the creator's "looks like Roblox" feedback.** Characters were rebuilt in src/gfx/charart/*: natural proportions (adult about 33 px with an 8 px head), five-tone hue-shifted ramps, form shading, selective outlines, manual anti-aliasing, and painterly portraits. Matches now render at native resolution, replacing D-007's 2× stage, with a dedicated 56–66 px side-view wrestler renderer (src/match/wrestler-art.ts), 28 fan variants, and five redesigned venues with beams, haze and bloom.
- **D-017 Characters are painted at double pixel density** (2026-10-07, after "still too simple and a little blocky"). The creator chose finer pixels over bigger characters: same on-screen size next to doors and buildings, with 4× the art pixels. The screen buffer is now 2× native (`DENSITY` in core/screen.ts) and every scene draws in native pixels under a base 2× transform, so world pixels still land on exactly `scale` device pixels at any zoom. Character sprites are authored at `CHAR_DENSITY` art pixels per native pixel (gfx/characters.ts) and drawn at 1/density; native-resolution offscreen canvases (VHS TV, Dungeon ghosts) get a smooth downscaled copy. Tradeoff accepted: character pixels are finer than the world's.
- **D-018 World art can be painted at double density too.** `dense(2, fn)` in gfx/kit.ts builds sprites into a 2× buffer while every primitive keeps taking world-pixel coordinates. Rects render identically, ellipses and polygons get smoother, and new detail goes on half-pixels (fractional coords, `P1`, and `FX`/`FY` inside paint callbacks). Paint callbacks still receive whole world-pixel x, y, so existing dithers don't change. Dense canvases are tagged, and `drawImage` draws them at logical size; use `lw()`/`lh()` for their logical size. Dense outlines are one world pixel thick. This makes people and places share one pixel grid again (resolving D-017's tradeoff).
- **D-019 World scale.** The creator said characters looked too big next to cars and buildings. Adults go from 33 to 30 px (heads unchanged, legs/torso/forearms shorter). Cars grow to roughly 64–70 px long. Buildings get taller floors and doors of about 32–36 px within their existing footprints.
- **D-020 The UI is made of in-world objects:** pixel-framed paper, enamel and brass in whole-pixel frames, with tokens defined once in src/ui/styles.css. No rounded pills, no saturated toy colours, no emoji icons (2026-10-07, after the creator called the UI "robloxy and child like").
- **D-021 Character animation is solved, not keyed** (2026-10-07, after "the walking looks rough... little to no animation that feels like a million bucks"). Walks and runs come from foot placement: each foot is planted for part of the cycle and slides back at exactly the body's travel speed, the hip rides as high as the planted legs allow (that gives contact, down, passing and up by itself) and knees come from two-bone IK. Front and back views reuse the same feet, mapped to screen depth and leg foreshortening. The walk is 8 frames, the run 6, and `Actor.phase` advances by distance moved rather than time, so feet stay planted; past a brisk cadence cap (about 3 cycles a second) the stride stretches instead, which only affects the fastest player movement. Personality comes from per-body gait styles in body.ts (heavy and giant lumber and sway, petite and kids spring, elders shuffle; elders also walk at 36 px/s instead of 42). Idle life is a seeded program (uneven breathing, weight shifts, glances, double blinks, and personal quirks such as Birdie's toe tap, Pip's bounce and Earl's glasses push) that is staggered per look so crowds never move in sync. Side-view far legs now use + = forward like the near leg, which fixes the match poses' back legs (strike, grapple, stagger).
- **D-022 Stardew's scale rules.** The camera aims for a 270-pixel-tall view (480×270 at 1080p) using the closest whole-number zoom. Doors are 1×2 tiles, a storey is about 3 tiles of wall, shops are 8–12 tiles wide and houses 6–8. Buildings become wide and low, with the town re-laid out to fit (docs/STARDEW_SCALE.md).
